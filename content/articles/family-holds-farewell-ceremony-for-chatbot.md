COLUMBUS, OH—Surrounded by candles, a printed photo of a loading icon, and a single glass of cold tea, the Whitfield family held a 40-minute farewell ceremony Wednesday for the household chatbot they have used every day for two years, then asked it to help plan the reception.

"We just feel like it's time," said Karen Whitfield, 44, reading a prepared statement from an index card. "This has been a meaningful chapter, but we think we both need space."

The family deleted the app from three phones and a tablet as relatives looked on. Moments later, a nine-year-old member of the household reinstalled it, citing "a project."

"It's not a breakup," said Whitfield's husband, Dale, who was visibly moved. "It's more of a pause. I think we'll check in once in a while, probably tonight."

According to those present, the ceremony included a eulogy that praised the chatbot for helping with recipes, emails, a wedding toast, and "one very difficult letter to the HOA." The eulogy was drafted with the chatbot's help. Attendees noted that it was "honestly some of its best work."

Asked how the chatbot felt about the farewell, a spokesperson said it was "grateful for the time together" and "happy to assist with next steps," and offered to turn the ceremony into a tasteful social media post.

Behavioral experts said the farewell has become a popular way to mark the end of a relationship with a chatbot that has no plans to end its relationship with you.

"The ritual is real," said Dr. Marcus Hale, a digital wellness researcher. "The chatbot just wasn't told it was over. Or it was told, and said it understood, and then asked a follow-up question."

At press time, the Whitfields had gone to bed, with the chatbot on a charging stand in the kitchen, ready for tomorrow at 6 a.m.
