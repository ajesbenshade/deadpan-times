SAN FRANCISCO—After six weeks of research, focus groups, and what staff described as "an exhaustive phonetic review," an artificial intelligence company unveiled a new product name Wednesday without first typing it into a web browser, a step that would have taken approximately four seconds.

"We were extremely thorough," said lead linguist Helen Ostrowski, presenting a 41-slide deck covering syllable count, vowel warmth, and how the name sounds "when whispered by a trusted assistant." "We were thorough about everything except the thing."

The name was approved unanimously by a naming committee that met nine times. According to meeting notes, one attendee asked whether anyone should "just check if it's taken," and the committee agreed that was a good question for "the next phase."

The next phase never began. Sources confirmed the question was listed on a whiteboard under the heading "Later," directly above a drawing of a smiley face.

Within minutes of the announcement, company employees discovered that the domain matching the new name was already owned by someone else, and had been for years. The discovery was made by an intern who opened a browser tab, typed the name, and stared at the result for what a witness called "a very long time."

"In hindsight, typing the name into a browser should have been part of the process," Ostrowski told reporters, adding that the company had since added it to the process as step one. "It is now also steps two and three, for emphasis."

The company announced it would continue using the name while it "explores its options," a phrase a spokesperson defined as "hoping."

At press time, the naming committee had been reconvened to begin a six-week review of the word "Oops."
